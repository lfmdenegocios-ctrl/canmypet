Can cats eat chewing gum?

Cats can eat chewing gum, but only in moderation.

Gum is a choking hazard and the xylitol it often contains is best kept far from cats.

Why this verdict for cats

Gum is a choking hazard and the xylitol it often contains is best kept far from cats.

Chewing gum is the single most common way pets get poisoned by xylitol — a sugar substitute that is harmless to us and dangerous to dogs at doses smaller than most owners imagine. A handbag left on a sofa is the classic scenario.

How much is safe?

There is no safe amount for a dog, and the maths is unforgiving. Blood-sugar collapse begins around 0.1 g of xylitol per kg of body weight, and liver failure around 0.5 g/kg. The complication is that gum brands vary enormously — some pieces contain around 0.3 g of xylitol, others closer to 1 g — so a handful of pieces can push a small dog into the liver-damage range. Xylitol also appears in sugar-free mints, some peanut butters, protein bars, chewable vitamins and toothpaste. Cats appear far less susceptible, but gum is still not something to allow.

What happens if a cat eats too much?

Xylitol tricks the pancreas into dumping insulin, so blood sugar crashes — typically within 10 to 60 minutes, though some products delay it for hours. That looks like weakness, staggering, trembling, vomiting, collapse and, if untreated, seizures. Liver injury, when it happens, tends to appear over the following one to three days as lethargy, appetite loss and yellowing gums. Separately, a swallowed wad of gum can obstruct a small dog regardless of what it is sweetened with.

⚠ What to do if your cat ate chewing gum

Check the wrapper for 'xylitol' or 'birch sugar' — it is sometimes listed only under sugar alcohols — then call a vet or poison line immediately (ASPCA 888-426-4435 · Pet Poison Helpline 855-764-7661) with the brand, the number of pieces and your dog's weight. This one moves fast enough that it is worth calling from the car. Do not try to raise blood sugar with food at home unless a vet tells you to; treatment needs monitored IV dextrose and liver protection, not a spoon of honey.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if the gum had xylitol?

Read the ingredient list rather than the front of the pack. It may be listed as xylitol, birch sugar, or grouped under 'sugar alcohols'. If you cannot find the wrapper, assume it did and call.

Is sugar-free gum without xylitol safe then?

Safer from a poisoning standpoint — sorbitol and maltitol mostly cause an upset stomach — but a swallowed piece can still cause a blockage in a small dog.

Are cats at risk from xylitol too?

Cats seem markedly less sensitive and cases are rare, but the data is thin. Keep gum away from them anyway.
